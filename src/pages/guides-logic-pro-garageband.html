Logic Pro and GarageBand for beginners

Both are Apple's music apps, and both are where a lot of artists start making beats and recording ideas. Here's which one to use, what they cost, and the skills worth learning first.

Our advice: start in GarageBand. Move to Logic Pro when you hit a limit you can name, for example when you need more control over editing vocals, automation or mixing.

The first 8 skills to learn, in order

  1. Set the tempo and key before you start. Everything else lines up to them.
  2. Loops. Apple Loops are a fast way to learn how arrangements are built. Swap in your own sounds as you go.
  3. Drummer. Both apps include virtual drummers that follow your song, which is great for getting a groove going quickly.
  4. Recording with a MIDI keyboard. Play parts in, then fix timing with quantize instead of re-recording.
  5. Recording vocals. Use headphones, record several takes, and keep the input level well below the red. Our session prep guide covers technique.
  6. Arranging. Copy sections to build intro, verse, hook and bridge, then take parts away to create contrast.
  7. Basic mixing. Balance volume and panning first. Effects come after the balance works.
  8. Exporting. Bounce a WAV for anyone mixing or mastering your song. For a studio mix, export each track as its own file (stems) from the same start point.

What to learn next in Logic Pro

  • Comping: build one perfect vocal from your best moments across several takes.
  • Flex Time and Flex Pitch: tighten timing and pitch without re-recording.
  • Automation: make volume, effects and filters move over time.
  • Smart Controls and the Step Sequencer: quick sound design and pattern-based beats.

When to bring it to a studio

Home sessions are great for writing and demos. A studio helps when the vocal sound matters, when you need a professional mix and master for release, or when you want someone to tell you honestly what's working.
