How to choose a recording studio near Baltimore

There are a lot of studios around Baltimore, from home setups to large commercial rooms. These are the questions worth asking before you spend money on any of them, including ours.

1. What does an hour actually cost, and what's included?

Peerspace reports that recording studios listed on its platform in Baltimore average about $69 an hour (checked November 2026).1 That figure doesn't tell you what you get for the money, so ask:

  • Is an engineer included, or is it a room rental where you run the session yourself?
  • Are mixing and mastering separate? They usually are.
  • Is there a minimum booking length, or a deposit or cancellation fee?

A studio that publishes its prices makes this easy to compare. Ours are on the pricing guide: $60 an hour to record, $75 for a mixing session and $50 for mastering.

2. Does the engineer fit your music?

The engineer matters more than the gear list. Listen to music they've worked on, and ask whether they work with your genre. A good engineer will also tell you honestly when a take isn't there yet.

3. Can you book when you actually create?

If you work a day job or write late at night, hours matter. Check whether the studio offers evening or overnight sessions and how far ahead you need to book. Disco Sundays members get 24/7 access, up to 4 hours a day.

4. What do real clients say?

Read recent reviews on Google, not just the testimonials a studio picks for its own website. Look for comments about patience, communication and whether files were delivered on time. You can read every one of our Google reviews.

5. Can you visit first?

A good studio lets you see the room, hear the monitors and meet the engineer before you pay. We offer a free 30-minute studio visit.

6. If you record often, is there a membership?

Paying by the hour suits one-off songs. If you're working on a project every week, a membership can cost far less. Our Unlimited membership is $200 a month plus Maryland tax. Compare it with hourly booking on the pricing guide.

7. Do you need more than studio time?

If you want to learn production, build your sound or plan a release, look for a studio that offers mentoring, not just a room. Our Artist Development Program covers production in Logic Pro and GarageBand, MIDI and recording technique, with mentorship from the team.
