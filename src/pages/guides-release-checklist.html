Song release checklist

Everything an independent artist needs to finish before a song comes out, in the order it needs doing. Print it, tick it off, or let the free release planner put real dates on every step.

6+ weeks before release

  • Final recording done, with every vocal, double and ad-lib you need. Not sure it's ready? Get free AI demo feedback.
  • Mix finished and approved. Compare it with a reference using the free mix comparison tool.
  • Master finished, plus a clean version if the song has explicit lyrics. See mixing vs mastering.
  • Split sheet signed by everyone who wrote or produced the song, showing who owns what percentage.
  • Beat license or producer agreement in writing if you didn't make the beat.
  • Artwork started. Most distributors ask for a square image of at least 3000 × 3000 pixels with no website addresses or social handles on it.

4 weeks before

  • Upload to your distributor with the release date set. Leave time for review; many distributors recommend several weeks.
  • Check the metadata: song title, artist name spelling, featured artists, songwriter and producer credits, explicit tag and genre.
  • ISRC code assigned. Most distributors create one for you.
  • Songwriters registered with a performing rights organization such as BMI or ASCAP, and the song registered there.
  • Mechanical royalties: make sure the song will be registered with The MLC, directly or through your publisher or administrator.
  • Master owner registered with SoundExchange to collect digital performance royalties.

1 to 3 weeks before

  • Pitch the song through Spotify for Artists. Pitches must go in at least 7 days before release, and earlier is better.
  • Pre-save link live and shared.
  • Content ready: clips for short-form video, a lyric video or visualizer, photos and a short story about the song.
  • Press and radio: send the song to local outlets and stations that take submissions from Maryland artists, with a short bio and the release date.
  • Release-week show or listening event booked, if you're doing one.

Release day

  • Check the song is live and correct on every platform.
  • Post, share the link everywhere and pin it to your profiles.
  • Thank everyone who worked on it, and tag them.

The 2 weeks after

  • Keep posting. Most songs don't take off on day one.
  • Watch your streaming dashboards to see which content brought listeners in.
  • Plan the next release while you have momentum.
