Mixing vs mastering: what's the difference?

They're two separate jobs, done in order, and a song meant for release needs both. Here's what each one does, what to send, and what to expect to pay.

The short version

Mixing turns your separate recordings (vocals, beat, instruments) into one balanced stereo song. Mastering takes that finished mix and prepares it for release, so it sounds consistent and competitive on phones, car speakers, headphones and streaming services.

Think of mixing as arranging everything inside the song, and mastering as the final polish on the whole song.

What happens in a mix

  • Balance: setting the level of every part so the vocal sits where it should against the beat.
  • EQ: cleaning up muddy or harsh frequencies so parts stop fighting each other. The kick and the bass, for example, often need room made for each other.
  • Compression: controlling how much a vocal or instrument jumps in volume, so every word is heard.
  • Space and depth: reverb, delay and stereo placement that make the song feel wide and three-dimensional.
  • Vocal production: timing, tuning where the style calls for it, ad-lib placement and effects.

A mix can only be as good as the recording. Fixing a weak recording in the mix costs time; re-recording a line is often faster.

What happens in mastering

  • Overall tone: small EQ moves on the whole song so it isn't too bright, dark or boomy.
  • Loudness: bringing the song up to a competitive level without crushing it.
  • Consistency: on an EP or album, making the songs sit together at a similar level and tone.
  • Delivery: a final high-quality file, checked for clipping, ready for your distributor.

How loud should a master be for streaming?

Streaming services turn loud songs down to a reference level, so squeezing out extra loudness doesn't make you louder on Spotify; it just costs you punch. Spotify's reference is around -14 LUFS integrated, and Apple Music's is around -16 LUFS. Many genres are mastered louder than that on purpose. What matters is that the master sounds good after normalization, not that it hits a number.

Can one person do both?

Yes, and on a budget that's common. A separate mastering pass can still help, because fresh ears catch things the mix engineer stopped hearing. Either way, always finish the mix before you master. Mastering can't fix a vocal that's buried in the mix.

Common questions

Do I need mastering if I'm only posting to social media?

A mastered file will sound more consistent next to everything else people are scrolling past, but for a quick snippet a good mix is often enough. For anything going to streaming services, master it.

Which comes first, mixing or mastering?

Always mixing. Mastering works on the finished stereo mix.

How long does it take?
